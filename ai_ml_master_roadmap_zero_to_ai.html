AI / ML Master Roadmap

The corrected version. This roadmap is deliberately built for someone who may start with weak mathematics and still wants to reach serious Deep Learning, modern AI, model-building, systems and research. DSA is now a first-class pillar instead of an afterthought.

Core rule: learn foundations in order, but do not wait years before building. Every stage has a build/test milestone.

0. What “complete” means here

This is not a list of every AI buzzword. It is a dependency-aware curriculum. You do not need to master every advanced topic before moving forward.

Must master

Math foundations, Python, discrete math, DSA, algorithms, statistics, ML, Deep Learning, PyTorch, experimentation, Git/Linux, software engineering and model training.

Must understand

OS, networking, databases, GPU/memory concepts, MLOps, distributed training, inference, security, RAG, agents, multimodal and evaluation.

Specialize later

RL research, CUDA kernels, advanced distributed systems, multimodal research, biological foundation models, generative biology and specialized architectures.

12. Research track — required for “build my own models”

Paper literacy

Read abstract → method → assumptions → experiments → ablations → limitations. Maintain structured notes.

Reproduction

Reimplement a manageable paper, publish code/configs, compare metrics, document deviations.

Experimentation

Hypothesis → baseline → controlled change → ablation → metric → uncertainty → conclusion.

Research engineering

Seeds, configs, checkpoints, data versions, logs, experiment tracking and reproducible environments.

15. What NOT to learn yet

  • Every new AI framework.
  • Every new model release.
  • Every cloud provider.
  • Thousands of LeetCode questions.
  • Advanced CUDA before you understand PyTorch performance.
  • Huge foundation-model training on a 4 GB VRAM laptop.
  • Certificates as a substitute for projects.
Your RTX 3050 4 GB is a learning machine, not a frontier-training cluster. Use it for small models, experiments, fine-tuning techniques, inference and systems learning; use cloud/HPC later when the experiment genuinely needs it.

15A. Final technical-completeness pass

These are the last deliberately included supporting topics. They are not new giant branches of the roadmap; they are compact skills that close practical gaps between mathematics, CS, ML and research.

Numerical computing

Floating-point error, numerical stability, conditioning, finite differences, numerical integration, Newton's method, iterative methods and numerical linear algebra.

Information theory

Entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence, mutual information, information gain and conditional entropy.

Learning theory

Bias/variance, empirical risk minimization, regularization, VC-dimension/PAC-learning intuition and generalization bounds.

Concurrency

Async I/O, asyncio, threads vs processes, queues, synchronization and race conditions.

AI data structures

LRU caching, inverted indexes, Bloom filters, approximate nearest-neighbor search and vector indexes.

Advanced algorithms

Randomized algorithms, approximation algorithms and a conceptual introduction to P, NP, reductions and NP-completeness.

Research statistics

Bootstrap, effect sizes, uncertainty across random seeds, multiple comparisons and statistical vs practical significance.

Architecture literacy

Parameter count, FLOPs, activation memory, attention complexity, compute-vs-memory bottlenecks, dense vs sparse/MoE tradeoffs.

Lock rule: after these topics are incorporated, do not keep expanding the roadmap just because a new framework, model release or AI trend appears. New topics belong in the specialization/research phase when they become relevant to an actual project.

17. The study loop

Learn → derive/explain → implement → solve → build → test → document → revisit.

For every major topic, keep four outputs: notes, code, questions, project evidence. The question bank and training lab below are designed around this loop.
